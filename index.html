Project background

Why the baseline was conducted and how the evidence is intended to support the Bulimbo Farmer Research Network.

Previous FRN experience: The mole Farmer Research Network (FRN) approach has already been practised and tested with farmers in Kakamega. Through this work, farmers and project teams gained experience with different mole-control approaches, including trapping, flushing/flooding and plant-based repellents such as simsim and tephrosia. The experience showed the value of practical demonstrations, farmer learning, follow-up and combining control options rather than relying on one method alone.

Why Bulimbo: Bulimbo is now being brought into the process to establish its own evidence base on the extent, severity and effects of mole infestation. This baseline provides the starting picture before intervention and helps the programme understand what farmers are already doing, what they know, where the main burden is located and what support they need.

What the Bulimbo FRN is intended to do: Use the baseline to identify farmers who can potentially become Mole Champions, sensitise and practically expose them to the mole-control methods already explored through the FRN, and then support farmer-led testing, observation, follow-up and learning within Bulimbo. The purpose is not simply to introduce a technology, but to build local capacity to test, compare, learn and share what works under Bulimbo conditions.

FRN pathway: Previous FRN learning → Bulimbo baseline → identify potential Mole Champions → sensitisation and practical learning → farmer-led testing → follow-up and evidence → reflection and learning.

Key findings

  • Mole infestation is widespread: 149 farmers reported current signs.
  • Field evidence confirms activity on 134 farms.
  • 137 farmers reported moderate, severe or very severe infestation.
  • 59 farmers reported high or very high crop loss.
  • Only 55 farmers had previously attempted control.
  • Only 9 farmers reported receiving mole identification/control training.

Priority interpretation

  • 149 farmers (92.5%) reported current infestation.
  • 134 farmers (83.2%) had active signs observed.
  • 137 farmers (85.1%) were in moderate, severe or very severe categories.
  • 64 farmers (39.8%) were severe or very severe.
  • 59 farmers (36.6%) reported high or very high crop loss.

Programme implication: severity and crop-loss indicators can be used together to prioritise farmers for practical demonstrations, follow-up and farmer-led testing.
